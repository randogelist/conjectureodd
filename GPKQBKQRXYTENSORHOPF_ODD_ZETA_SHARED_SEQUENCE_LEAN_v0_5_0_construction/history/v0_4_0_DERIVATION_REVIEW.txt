OBJECTS AND CERTIFIED BASELINE
For a fixed k>=2 and admissible p=(m,a,b), put N=a*t, K=b*t,
h=(b-a)*t, t>=1. L is the absolute leading coefficient of the canonical
primitive integer polynomial; R is the actual Gram/slope determinant ratio.
E=L*R=P_t(zeta(2*k+1))>0 and deg(P_t)=h. These actual-family identities,
positivity, and the conditional irrationality transfers are in the retained
Lean sources. The user reported v0.3.0 native BUILD=PASS and independent
axiom-audit PASS, with 39 modules reused. This does not certify the new
v0.4.0 router before its own native run.

POSITIVE PAPER ESTIMATES RECHECKED
1. On p=(1,1,2), for every fixed k>=2:
     log R_(k,t) <= -delta*t^2 + O_k(t*log(t+1)),
     delta = 2*log(pi/2) + (9/2)*log(3) > 5.
   Hence R_(k,t)<=exp(-5*t^2) eventually. The argument uses the positive
   Gram integral, q(y^2)<=(t!)^4/((2t)!)^2 * exp(pi*y), Hadamard's
   inequality, the exact slope product, and factorial asymptotics.

2. For m=1, with ell=lcm(1,...,2*K+2*k+2):
     L divides [(2*k)! * ell^(4*k+3)]^h.
   Thus, on p=(1,1,2), limsup log(L_(k,t))/t^2 <= 16*k+12.
   In particular the available weight-five upper bound is 44.
   The integer-valued-polynomial proof uses the Bernoulli functional with
   B_1=-1/2. Actual ZH moments involve only even Bernoulli indices >=2;
   a future Lean formalization must make that convention agreement explicit.

3. At FIXED geometry, for every fixed k>=1:
     R_(k+1)/R_k <= ((2*k)!)^h *
       [(4*m*N+2*h+2*k+3)/(N+1)]^(2*h).
   Along N=a*t,K=b*t this is at most exp(C_(k,p)*t), with finite C_(k,p).
   This controls R only. It neither bounds the primitive L ratio nor
   compares different geometries p_k and p_(k+1).

RATE DIRECTION SAFEGUARDS
To prove a strict gap, compare a HEIGHT UPPER rate with a DECAY LOWER rate:
     upper(log L/t^2) < lower(-log R/t^2).
To exclude a strict gap, compare a HEIGHT LOWER rate with a DECAY UPPER rate.
Failure of the first comparison is only INCONCLUSIVE, not exclusion.
Thus the available bounds 44 and delta~=5.84692 do not show that the
weight-five profile fails. They also do not prove that it succeeds.
The number 44 is a coarse upper bound, not the established true height rate.

INVARIANTS MUST REMAIN DISTINCT
W(k,p): for every integer q>=2 there is t>=1 with
        0<E_(k,p)(t)<q^(-(b-a)*t).
QD(k,p): there are epsilon>0 and T such that
         E_(k,p)(t)<=exp(-epsilon*t^2) for all t>=T.
ProfileGap(k,p): separate eventual bounds L<=exp(A*t^2),
                R<=exp(-B*t^2), with 0<=A<B.
Valid implications: ProfileGap -> QD -> W -> irrationality at that weight.
QD does not automatically imply ProfileGap: oscillations in L and R can
prevent separated rates even when their product decays quadratically.
Finite quadratic height growth alone does not repair this implication.

A product transfer E_next(t)<=exp(C*t)*E_current(t)^theta, theta>0,
preserves both QD and W. A loss exp(o(t^2)) preserves QD but need not
preserve W. Separate factor transfers preserve ProfileGap when their powers
and rate losses are compatible. Index resampling is not automatic for W:
its successful indices could miss the chosen subsequence.

PAPER-ONLY EXCLUSION GUARDS RECHECKED
Let alpha=a/b and lambda=1-alpha. Success in W, and therefore success in
QD or ProfileGap, requires m*alpha<5. If m*alpha>=5, the interval Gram
lower bound and AM-GM slope bound give
     liminf log(R)/K^2 >= (3/28)*lambda > 0,
and L>=1 prevents E from being small on unbounded indices.

For k>=108, the strengthened prime-denominator argument additionally gives
     W(k,p) -> m>=k+1 and 0<alpha<5/m<=5/(k+1).
The argument compares a lower height rate against the upper analytic decay
budget <(587/49)*lambda<12*lambda. It first excludes k>=2*m-1 using
the prime interval integral, then excludes the remaining m<=k using the
sharper collision bound once alpha<1/11.
These are reviewed PAPER deductions. They must not be represented as
Lean-certified rejection guards without explicit formal proofs. They show
why an all-weight successful construction cannot retain one fixed geometry.

OPEN OBLIGATIONS AND ROUTING
Base-height route: prove, at p_2=(1,1,2), an eventual height bound with
0<=A<delta. This is mathematically open.
Analytic-base route: formalize the paper estimate in item 1 as the exact
AnalyticBaseInput. The v0.3.0 height endpoint still assumes that input.
Direct-product route: a proof of actual QD at the base can replace the two
base estimates; no such successful base has been constructed here.
Induction route: construct admissible profiles and prove the actual product
transfer, including primitive normalization and any geometry change. This
remains mathematically open. An R-only comparison cannot close this route.
Apéry's theorem supplies weight three as a separate explicit input.

No rate or threshold uniform in k is asserted or needed: every induction
step concerns a fixed k. No single finite denominator witness establishes
irrationality; it excludes that denominator only. No numerical scan or
candidate profile is a proof of an all-t estimate.
